# Strategy and Positioning

## Brand foundation

Define the smallest useful set of strategic decisions.

### Purpose
Why the organization matters beyond the immediate transaction. Keep it believable and connected to actual behavior.

### Vision
The future state the brand wants to help create.

### Mission
What the organization does, for whom, and how it advances that future now.

### Values
Prefer 3–5 values that can guide real decisions. For each value include a behavioral meaning. Avoid generic words without consequences.

### Audience
Prioritize. A brand for “everyone” has no useful center. Capture:
- who matters most
- their situation / job-to-be-done
- desired progress
- barriers / anxieties
- decision criteria
- language they naturally use

### Competitive frame
State the category or alternative set the brand wants to be compared against. This affects what customers expect and what the identity must signal.

### Value proposition
Use this internal logic:
**For [audience], [brand/offer] helps [outcome] by [distinct mechanism/advantage], unlike [alternative], because [proof].**

Do not force the final public copy to sound like this template.

### Positioning statement
A strong internal positioning statement contains:
- target audience
- category/frame
- point of difference
- customer benefit
- reason to believe

### Brand promise
One clear expectation the brand repeatedly commits to delivering. It must be supportable by operations/product reality.

### Reasons to believe
List tangible proof: capabilities, process, expertise, technology, sourcing, data, credentials, service model, guarantees, results, or other substantiated evidence.

### Personality
Choose 3–5 traits with behavioral definitions and opposites. Example structure:
- **Confident, not arrogant** — decisive language; no chest-beating.
- **Technical, not cryptic** — expert thinking explained clearly.

### Brand essence
Optional. A short internal organizing idea, not automatically a tagline.

## Brand architecture

When multiple offers/brands exist, explicitly choose or document a model:
- branded house
- endorsed brand
- house of brands
- hybrid

Define naming, logo lockup, color, messaging and endorsement relationships before creating sub-brand identities.

## Strategy test

A strategy is ready when it can answer:
1. Why should this brand exist?
2. Who is it primarily for?
3. What space does it want to own?
4. Why choose it over alternatives?
5. What can it credibly prove?
6. How should it feel and behave?
7. What future growth must the identity accommodate?

If these answers contradict one another, do not move to visual design yet.
