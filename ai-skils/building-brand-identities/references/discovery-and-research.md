# Discovery and Research

## Goal

Turn an idea into a decision-ready brief. Do not begin naming or design from vague adjectives alone.

## Minimum discovery fields

Capture what is known and mark unknowns explicitly:

1. **Business / offer** — what is sold, to whom, how it works, business model, price position.
2. **Problem / job** — what customer problem, desire, or job the brand serves.
3. **Audience** — primary, secondary, buyer vs user, demographics only when useful, behaviors, motivations, objections, cultural context.
4. **Market** — geography, language, category, maturity, relevant regulations or sensitivities.
5. **Competition** — direct, indirect, substitutes, category leaders, visual/verbal conventions.
6. **Difference** — capabilities, proof, unfair advantages, experience, point of view.
7. **Ambition** — what the brand should become in 3–5 years; products/markets likely to be added.
8. **Personality intent** — desired and undesired perceptions.
9. **Constraints** — fixed name, legacy equity, founder requirements, domains, colors, typography, accessibility, production, budget, platforms.
10. **Touchpoints** — website/app, social, sales, packaging, signage, events, uniforms, print, decks, ads, email, etc.
11. **Legal scope** — target jurisdictions and goods/services categories for preliminary naming checks.
12. **Success** — what would make the identity strategically successful, not merely liked.

## Questions policy

Ask questions in priority order. A question is worth asking when its answer can change positioning, naming, architecture, language, or creative direction. Avoid interview theater.

When the user requests immediate work with incomplete information:
- state the important assumptions;
- create a provisional version;
- identify which decisions should be revisited when missing evidence arrives.

## Research frame

Research should answer decisions, not collect trivia.

### Category scan
- common claims and taglines
- dominant visual conventions and clichés
- naming patterns
- customer language in reviews/forums/searches when available
- price/value signals
- whitespace and overused themes

### Competitor matrix

For each meaningful competitor, capture:
- target audience
- positioning / promise
- core messages
- name type
- visual signals
- strengths
- weaknesses / sameness
- opportunity for differentiation

### Evidence discipline

Maintain three buckets:
- **Facts:** supported by user input or reliable sources.
- **Inferences:** reasoned conclusions from facts.
- **Assumptions:** unverified placeholders used to keep progress moving.

Do not turn an assumption into a brand claim.

## Research output

Summarize:
- category patterns
- customer tensions
- competitive whitespace
- opportunities
- risks
- strategic implications for name, voice and visual identity
