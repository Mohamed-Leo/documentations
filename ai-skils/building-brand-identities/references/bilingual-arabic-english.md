# Arabic / English Bilingual Branding

Use this reference in addition to the normal strategy, naming, verbal and visual workflows.

## Naming

For each serious candidate evaluate:
- Arabic meaning and connotations
- English meaning and accidental associations
- pronunciation by Arabic speakers
- pronunciation by English speakers
- transliteration options
- likely spelling variants in search and word of mouth
- whether diacritics are essential to intended pronunciation/meaning
- readability without diacritics
- plural/verb/noun ambiguity if relevant
- dialect sensitivities in target Arab markets

Choose one canonical Arabic form and one canonical Latin spelling. Document accepted and rejected spelling variants.

## Verbal identity

Do not translate marketing copy literally by default. Preserve intent, positioning, voice and emotional effect.

Create a terminology table for:
- brand name
- descriptor
- product names
- core features/services
- recurring CTAs
- key category words

Specify whether numerals, punctuation and English technical terms remain Latin or are localized.

## Typography

Evaluate Arabic and Latin together in actual lockups and layouts. Align perceived weight, visual density and hierarchy rather than numeric font sizes alone.

Check:
- Arabic shaping and ligatures
- glyph quality across required weights
- Arabic numerals / Latin numerals policy
- punctuation
- line height
- mixed-direction strings
- UI truncation
- webfont performance and licensing

## Logo lockups

Decide whether the primary system is:
- Arabic-first
- English-first
- equal bilingual
- market-dependent

Create lockups intentionally. Do not simply place two wordmarks near each other without hierarchy, spacing and alignment rules.

## Layout

For RTL contexts, mirror layout logic when meaning and reading flow require it, but do not mechanically mirror logos, photos, graphs, media controls, or directional symbols when doing so changes meaning.

Test bilingual identity on:
- mobile header/nav
- social avatar + profile name
- business card
- presentation cover
- invoice/official document if relevant
- packaging/signage when relevant

## Cultural review

For brands operating across the Middle East, avoid treating “Arabic market” as one homogeneous audience. Validate important names, idioms, colors, symbols and campaign lines in the actual countries/dialects that matter.
