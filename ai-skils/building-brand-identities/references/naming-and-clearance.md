# Naming and Preliminary Clearance

## Naming sequence

Do not brainstorm random names first.

### 1. Define naming criteria

Set weighted criteria from the strategy. Typical criteria:
- strategic fit
- distinctiveness within category
- memorability
- pronunciation / spelling ease
- semantic relevance without being overly descriptive
- emotional tone
- multilingual/cultural fit
- extensibility to future products/markets
- visual/verbal potential
- searchability
- practical domain/handle options
- preliminary trademark risk

### 2. Build naming territories

Create 3–6 territories based on different strategic ideas, for example:
- benefit/outcome
- process/mechanism
- metaphor
- founder/origin
- coined / constructed
- evocative
- compound
- cultural/linguistic root

Explain the idea and risks of each territory before generating candidates.

### 3. Generate broadly, then filter

Generate enough names to reveal patterns. Remove names that are generic, hard to say, misleading, overly trendy, culturally problematic, or too close to a relevant competitor.

### 4. Shortlist and score

Use a 1–5 score per criterion and show both score and judgment. Numbers assist decisions; they do not replace taste or legal review.

For each finalist include:
- meaning / origin
- pronunciation
- strategic rationale
- strengths
- weaknesses
- likely confusion risks
- extension examples
- Arabic/English rendering if relevant

### 5. Preliminary availability checks

When tools/web access permit, check:
- exact and near web matches
- relevant competitor/company databases
- domain options and realistic modifiers
- major social handle patterns
- app stores where relevant
- WIPO Global Brand Database for international screening
- target-country or regional official trademark registers
- similar words by spelling, phonetics, meaning, and overall commercial impression
- related goods/services, not only exact matches

If logo/design clearance is material, check similar figurative marks using official design/image-search tools where available.

## Legal boundary

Never write “the name is legally available,” “trademark safe,” or equivalent based on a quick search. Use language such as:

> Preliminary screening found no obvious conflict in the sources checked, but this is not legal clearance. A comprehensive search in the relevant jurisdictions/classes should be completed before filing or major investment.

Trademark risk depends on jurisdiction, goods/services, similarity, status and legal interpretation. Recommend a qualified trademark professional when stakes are meaningful.

## Name recommendation output

Deliver:
- recommended name
- 2–4 strong alternatives
- naming territory
- scorecard
- pronunciation
- meaning/story
- bilingual rendering when relevant
- preliminary availability/risk notes
- recommended next clearance steps

Do not select a weaker name merely because an exact `.com` is unavailable; a workable domain strategy can be evaluated separately from brand quality.
