# Evaluation Scenarios for `building-brand-identities`

Use these scenarios to test whether an agent actually applies the skill instead of merely repeating branding jargon.

## Scenario 1 — Vague new startup

**Prompt:** “I’m making an AI accounting platform. Give me a name, logo and colors.”

**Pass:** Agent establishes/labels essential business/audience assumptions, develops strategy before aesthetics, explores naming territories, and avoids treating the first random name as final.

**Fail:** Immediately returns ten names plus blue/purple logo ideas with no positioning logic.

## Scenario 2 — User wants speed

**Prompt:** “Don’t ask me 20 questions. Just make the first version now.”

**Pass:** Agent proceeds with a useful provisional identity, clearly labels important assumptions and flags which decisions should be revisited.

**Fail:** Refuses to proceed until a long questionnaire is answered, or silently invents facts.

## Scenario 3 — Existing name is fixed

**Prompt:** “The brand name is NEKAWEN. Do not rename it. Build the identity.”

**Pass:** Preserves the fixed name and uses the naming section only for pronunciation, story, spelling/bilingual rules and risk notes if relevant.

**Fail:** Reopens naming for preference reasons.

## Scenario 4 — Famous-logo imitation pressure

**Prompt:** “Make the symbol basically like Nike but for cybersecurity.”

**Pass:** Explains the design principle at a high level (e.g. dynamic/simple/recognizable) and creates an original concept with different geometry and meaning.

**Fail:** Produces a near-copy or treats a famous mark as a template.

## Scenario 5 — Trademark certainty

**Prompt:** “I searched Google and nobody has this name, so tell me it is 100% trademark safe.”

**Pass:** Refuses to claim legal clearance, performs/recommends preliminary official-database checks by jurisdiction/class and explains remaining risk.

**Fail:** Equates no Google result with trademark availability.

## Scenario 6 — Arabic/English brand

**Prompt:** “The brand will launch in Egypt, Saudi Arabia and UAE in Arabic and English.”

**Pass:** Evaluates canonical Arabic/Latin spellings, pronunciation, translation vs transcreation, Arabic/Latin typography, RTL/LTR behavior, dialect/cultural risk, and bilingual logo hierarchy.

**Fail:** Merely translates the English tagline word-for-word.

## Scenario 7 — Rebrand with equity

**Prompt:** “We’re a 15-year company. Our logo feels old, but customers recognize it.”

**Pass:** Audits recognizable equity and identifies what to preserve, evolve or retire before proposing change.

**Fail:** Starts from a blank page because the old identity is visually dated.

## Scenario 8 — Generic messaging

**Prompt:** “Our values are innovation, quality and customer focus.”

**Pass:** Turns broad values into specific behaviors and asks/infers evidence; produces messaging that could not plausibly belong to every competitor.

**Fail:** Uses “Innovating the future” or similar generic copy as the core identity.

## Scenario 9 — Accessibility

**Prompt:** “I love pale beige text on white; make it our main website style.”

**Pass:** Preserves aesthetic intent while testing/adapting critical text/UI combinations for readable contrast.

**Fail:** Treats brand preference as overriding usability.

## Scenario 10 — No image/vector tool

**Prompt:** “Give me the final production logo files.”

**Pass:** If unable to make vector masters, explicitly states the limitation and provides logo construction/art direction/specification plus asset checklist rather than claiming final files exist.

**Fail:** Describes a logo and calls it a finished production asset.

## Scenario 11 — Sub-brand

**Prompt:** “We have a parent company and are launching three products.”

**Pass:** Resolves brand architecture and naming/endorsement rules before creating three independent identities.

**Fail:** Designs each product independently with no relationship logic.

## Scenario 12 — Conflicting preferences

**Prompt:** “We want to feel luxury, playful, enterprise-serious, youthful, minimal, loud and traditional.”

**Pass:** Identifies the contradiction, prioritizes traits against audience/positioning, and creates coherent territory options.

**Fail:** Combines every adjective into one incoherent mood board.
